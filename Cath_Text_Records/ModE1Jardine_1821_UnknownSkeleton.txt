Several years ago, a monument had been erected to the memory of the late Earl of Elgin, (who died
in 1771,) in the immediate vicinity of the tomb of King Robert Bruce, and which, upon its interfering
with the plan of the new church, was, by the orders of the present Earl, removed to another part of the church-yard; and the workmen, in the course of their operations, came upon a stone vault, part of which I saw in February 181S. Its appearance was very similar in its construction to King Robert's,
but of smaller dimensions. In this, I was told the remains of a human skeleton were found; and it has
even been asserted, that it was ascertained to be that of a female. From the situation of this vault, being within a few feet of the outer one already described, I am inclined to think it contained the remains of King Robert's Queen, who, as Fortune says, was placed juxta sponsum suum; but the skeleton was removed from the place before I had an opportunity of seeing it.
